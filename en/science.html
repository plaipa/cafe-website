Inside you

From the first sip to bedtime

  1. ~10 min

    Absorption begins

    Caffeine passes quickly from the stomach and gut into the blood; almost all of it is absorbed within about 45 minutes.

  2. 30–60 min

    The peak

    Blood levels reach their highest point, and alertness is at its strongest.

  3. ~5 hours

    Half of it is gone

    The liver breaks caffeine down gradually. In adults the half-life is usually somewhere between 2 and 8 hours, and it varies from person to person.

  4. 6 h before bed

    The sleep line

    Studies have found that caffeine taken even 6 hours before bed can make sleep shorter and lighter.

Myth or fact

Three common beliefs about coffee

  • Coffee dehydrates you.

    For regular coffee drinkers, moderate amounts don’t dehydrate; coffee counts toward your daily fluids.

  • Coffee can replace sleep.

    Caffeine only masks the tiredness signal for a while. Adenosine keeps building up, and the need for sleep is still there.

  • Espresso has the most caffeine.

    Espresso is more concentrated, but a mug of filter coffee usually holds more caffeine in total than a single shot.

This page is general information, not medical advice. The chart uses a simple model (absorption within about 45 minutes, a 5-hour half-life); real numbers vary a lot between people. For example, the half-life is shorter in smokers and longer during pregnancy. The 400 mg daily limit applies to most healthy adults and is lower for children, teenagers, pregnant people and those with certain conditions.
